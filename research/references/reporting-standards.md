# Reporting standards

Every design has a reporting standard, and reviewers check against it. Choosing
one at submission time means reconstructing decisions that were made months
earlier. Choose it while the design is still open, and the checklist becomes a
plan rather than a repair.

The EQUATOR Network indexes these; the ones below are the ones that appear in
mental health, ageing and health services journals.

## By design

| Design | Guideline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Randomised trial | CONSORT | Extensions exist for pilot and feasibility trials, cluster designs, non-inferiority, and eHealth |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT | Register before recruitment; the registration is cited in every later paper |
| Cohort, case-control, cross-sectional | STROBE | Covers most of the survey and record-linkage work in this field |
| Qualitative interviews and focus groups | COREQ | 32 items; reflexivity and the coding process are the ones usually under-reported |
| Qualitative, broader designs | SRQR | Use where COREQ's assumptions do not fit |
| Systematic review | PRISMA 2020 | Add PRISMA-S for the search itself |
| Scoping review | PRISMA-ScR | The difference from a systematic review is the question, not the rigour |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS 2022 | Perspective, horizon, price year, discount rate all become required items |
| Prediction model | TRIPOD | |
| Diagnostic accuracy | STARD | |
| Quality improvement | SQUIRE 2.0 | |
| Case report | CARE | |
| Any study reporting new data, in a psychology or social-science journal | APA JARS | Title and abstract, participant characteristics, sampling, sample-size justification, measures, participant flow, and missing data handled explicitly |
| Meta-analysis | APA MARS, alongside PRISMA 2020 | Adds the coding of studies, the effect measure and the model choice to what PRISMA already asks |

A completed checklist with page numbers is increasingly required at submission.
Where an item does not apply, say why in the checklist. An item left blank is
read as an omission, not a judgement call.

APA's Journal Article Reporting Standards are worth reading even where the
target journal is not an APA journal, because they make four things explicit
that most manuscripts leave implicit: how the sample size was determined (a
power analysis, or the method used to establish precision), which measures were
collected but not reported, the flow of participants through every stage, and
the handling of missing data with its assumed mechanism. Reviewers ask for
these whether or not a checklist names them.

Mixed-methods reporting is the weakest-covered design on this list. There is no
guideline for it with the standing of CONSORT or PRISMA; check the EQUATOR
Network for the current entry before naming one, and in the meantime state the
design, the priority of the strands, the point at which they were integrated,
and what happened when they disagreed (`design-and-questions.md`).

## Describing the intervention

TIDieR is the standard for this, and it is the part of mental health intervention
papers most often too thin to replicate. The items: brief name, rationale,
materials, procedures, who delivered it, mode of delivery, location, when and how
much, tailoring, modifications, and fidelity.

For a psychological intervention, three additions matter in practice:

- **Who delivered it and with what training**, since a clinical psychologist and a
  trained lay facilitator are not the same intervention even with an identical
  manual.
- **Dose delivered versus dose received.** Sessions offered is not sessions
  attended, and attendance is not engagement with the content.
- **Fidelity and competence**, assessed separately. A therapist can follow the
  manual precisely and deliver it poorly.

For guided digital or online delivery, report what human support accompanied the
programme, how often, and by whom. Self-guided and guided versions of the same
content have different effects, and pooling them as "digital ACT" loses the
distinction that matters most.

## Numbers that must reconcile

Reviewers check these first because a mismatch is unambiguous:

- the CONSORT or PRISMA flow counts reconcile with the analysed sample
- every participant excluded between enrolment and analysis has a stated reason
- the registration's primary outcome matches the paper's primary outcome
- subgroup and secondary analyses are labelled as such, including those added
  after registration
- denominators are stated for every percentage

Where the primary outcome changed from the protocol, say so and why. A disclosed
change is a limitation; an undisclosed one is a problem.

## Practical habit

Before writing the results, list the guideline's items and note where each will
come from. Reading your own checklist against the draft is faster than reading
the draft against the checklist, and it catches the item that has no home - which
is usually the one a reviewer asks about.
