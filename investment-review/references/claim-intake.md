# The claim intake

Section 1 of the review. Most reviews are decided here, before any valuation.

## Four sources, in the order to reach for them

| # | Source | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What the household already wrote | The plan's decision record |
| 2 | What the household says now | The three questions below |
| 3 | What a source says | A post, a note, an article — quoted verbatim |
| 4 | What the price implies | Reverse-engineered from the multiple |

**And a fifth that must not be skipped: the assistant's own prior claims.** A
recommendation made last week is a claim like any other.

### The three questions

> What do you expect to happen? By when? What would show you were wrong?

Prediction, date, falsifier. Ask "why do you like it?" and you get a story; ask
these three and you get something that can be tested.

### Reverse-engineering from the price

When nobody has stated a thesis, read it off the multiple. **What must be true
for this price to be justified?** That is the market's implied claim, and it can
be reviewed even though nobody wrote it down.

## The three states

**Explicit and checkable** — review it. Specific claims with numbers and dates
are the only ones worth engaging.

**Vague or implicit** — restate it as a prediction with a date, then review the
restatement. **Label the restatement as one.** "I believe in its technology"
becomes "a margin advantage competitors cannot replicate within eighteen months,
measured by gross margin, by the 2028 accounts."

**Absent** — write that first. An absent claim is not a gap in the review; it is
the most useful output the review produces, because it says what is missing
before any money moves.

## A decision is not a claim

"Held for life, no exit condition" is a **decision**. Written that way it cannot
fail. The claim underneath it would be something like "the research spending
will produce durable competitive advantage" — which can be tested, and which
writes its own exit condition.

**Separate the two every time.** A decision dressed as a claim is
unfalsifiable, and an unfalsifiable position is one that can never be left on
evidence.

## Worked example one — four sources, disagreeing

A single-stock review where the case was made by a stranger and the household's
own claim was weaker than the stranger's.

| Source | Quality | Stands on |
|---|---|---|
| An external post | **Specific and checkable.** Predicted research spending by year, a depreciation fall, and a doubling of profit — two of the three are facts in filed accounts | Someone else's work |
| The household's own claim | **Vague.** Four claims bundled, no dates, no numbers | Belief |
| The decision | **Unfalsifiable** — "held for life, no exit condition" | Nothing recorded |
| The price | **Contradicted the post.** The multiple implied modest growth, not the doubling claimed | The market |

**The finding:** the strongest version of the case for holding was written by a
stranger. The household's own claim was less rigorous, and the decision
underneath it could not fail.

**And the mechanism did not support its conclusion.** The post argued for
doubled profit *and* for a transition to low-capex, low-growth maturity. Mature
capital-intensive manufacturers trade on single-digit multiples, so the two
arguments could not both pay.

## Worked example two — the intake catches the assistant's own error

The only stated claim was the assistant's.

| # | Claim | Testable? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A trailing yield of about 6.5% holds | Next dividend declaration | Checkable, not checked |
| 2 | A book multiple of 0.75 is cheap | Peers: 1.02 and 0.41 | Cheaper than one peer, not the other |
| 3 | Being further below its peak means a better entry | Forward returns | **Not checkable — and already refuted** |

**Claim 3 was the load-bearing one, and the household's own plan had already
tested and rejected it** — 36 years of daily data showed buying at a five-year
high produced better forward returns than buying on any other day.

**And the look-through ended the review.** The exposure already existed inside a
dividend fund at rank 6, at a wider spread than the single name offered. No
money moved.

## What the intake produces

A first section that answers four things before anything is valued:

1. Whose claim is this?
2. Is it checkable?
3. Does it address the price?
4. Does the household already own it?

**In both worked examples, those four questions decided the review.** Neither
needed a valuation model.
