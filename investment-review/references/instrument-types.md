# Instrument types

Section 0 of the review. Read only the bundle that applies.

Each instrument needs something established before the claim can be reviewed.
Getting this wrong is how two similarly named things get treated as one — an
index and a fund tracking it are not the same review, and a deposit is not a
security at all.

## Single share

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Listing, exchange, share class, currency, ADR or H-share vs A-share |
| **Where concentration hides** | The company itself. One business, one management, one country |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | Governance, related-party transactions, dilution, capital allocation, supplier terms |
| **What the review adds** | Share classes matter: the same company can trade at two prices. An H-share at a discount to its A-share yields more for the same dividend. That gap persists because of capital controls, not because it is waiting to close |

**Check the share class before comparing anything.** A fund holding the A-share
and a direct H-share position are the same company at different prices.

## Index

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Index code, **provider**, board, weighting method, capitalisation vs equal-weight |
| **Where concentration hides** | Sector weights, top-ten share, single-company caps. **"Index" sounds diversified and is not necessarily** |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | Reconstruction and rebalancing, methodology changes, a provider retiring the index |
| **What the review adds** | The provider determines everything. Two indices with similar names on different boards have different listing rules and different sectors |

**The composition is the load-bearing fact** and it is the one most often
missing. A 50-stock index with roughly half its weight in one sector is not
diversification; the name conceals it.

**Indices are absent from some sources entirely.** The China Securities Index
Company carries CSI-series codes and does not carry exchange-series ones, so an
index can be well documented in one place and invisible in another.

## Fund and ETF

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Fund, share class (A vs C), pool size, tracking index, management and service fees |
| **Where concentration hides** | In the underlying index, plus tracking error |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | Liquidation if the pool falls below the regulatory floor, manager change, tracking drift, redemption terms |
| **What the review adds** | Read the **fee schedule** rather than the headline. A class with no entry fee and an annual service fee is cheaper than a front-loaded class for short holds and dearer for long ones — the crossover is usually a few months |

**A feeder fund's holdings list shows its own direct sleeve, not the underlying
ETF.** A top-holding table showing 0.06% of net assets is describing the
remainder, not the portfolio. Do not read exposure off it as though it were the
whole.

**Pool size is a real check.** Below a regulatory floor, sustained outflows force
liquidation — which returns net asset value rather than losing money, but exits
the holder on a date they did not choose.

## Deposit and savings account

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Institution, account, **the balance cap the bonus applies to**, each qualifying category, and how long each lasts |
| **Where concentration hides** | The counterparty. Deposits are senior to equity but they are the same institution |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | Tier lapse, promotional expiry, a balance-increase requirement, withdrawal terms |
| **What the review adds** | This is the most commonly misread instrument in the whole set. See the traps below |

**The three traps, all found in live use:**

1. **The headline rate is not the effective rate.** A published maximum often
   applies only at a specified balance and to the first tranche of it. An
   account advertising a tier rate can pay a fraction of it at the balance
   actually held.
2. **Annualising a monthly figure from a 31-day month overstates a year** by
   roughly 3%. Check the day-count convention the calculator uses.
3. **A qualifying purchase may only last twelve months.** A bonus category
   bought once can lapse while the money that unlocked it stays locked for
   longer — which reverses the arithmetic of the purchase entirely.

## Bond, Savings Bond and T-bill

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Issuer, tenor, coupon or step-up structure, redemption terms, minimum |
| **Where concentration hides** | The issuer, and the fact that this is the same government risk as the currency |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | Reinvestment risk at maturity, and the rate you roll into |
| **What the review adds** | The redemption schedule is the product. A bond redeemable monthly in any amount is a liquidity instrument; one locked for thirty months is not, whatever the yield |

## Leveraged, inverse or derivative product

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | The multiple, the reset frequency, the decay mechanism, the issuer |
| **Where concentration hides** | Not in the holding — in the **notional exposure**, which is a multiple of the money at risk |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | **Decay.** A leveraged product can lose money while the underlying goes nowhere. Daily-reset products are not buy-and-hold instruments |
| **What the review adds** | Report the **notional** as well as the value. A position of a few per cent of a portfolio can be a double-digit exposure |

**If the household's mandate forbids leverage, this review has one output: the
holding is outside the rules, and that is a breach rather than a view.** Say so
first, before any analysis of the instrument.

## Structured product

| | |
|---|---|
| **Identify** | Issuer, tenor, the guarantee and exactly what it is on, the payout conditions, and the reason it is being bought |
| **Where concentration hides** | The issuer's credit, and the lock |
| **What can go wrong that isn't price** | The guarantee is only as good as the issuer; the payout may depend on a range the underlying never reaches |
| **What the review adds** | **Ask what it is for.** A product bought to qualify for a deposit account's bonus tier must be judged against the value of that tier and the duration of the qualification — not against its own yield |

## What every bundle shares

Four questions, adapted to the instrument:

1. **What is it, precisely?** Name, provider, class, currency.
2. **Where does the concentration actually sit?** Rarely where the label suggests.
3. **What can go wrong that is not the price?** Structure, counterparty, terms, decay.
4. **What does the review add that a quote does not?** If the answer is nothing, say so.
