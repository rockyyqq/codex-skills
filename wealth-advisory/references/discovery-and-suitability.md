# Discovery and suitability

Read this before projecting anything. A projection is only as good as the picture of the household behind it, and the picture is usually wrong in the first conversation.

## What must be established

| Area | Establish | Why it changes the plan |
|---|---|---|
| Household | Ages, marital status, dependants and their ages, residency and citizenship status, general health and family longevity | Citizenship/residency determines CPF and SRS eligibility; dependants change the insurance and liquidity requirement |
| Goals | Each goal with a date and a cost: retirement income, property, education, care, legacy, supporting parents | Goals without dates and costs cannot be funded or tested |
| Resources | Income and its stability and growth prospects; investment accounts; CPF balances by account; SRS balance; property; business interests; expected inheritances | A bonus or inheritance is not a plan. Treat it as upside, never as a funding source |
| Obligations | Debt and its rates, guarantees given, ongoing support payments, school fees, insurance premiums | Debt at a rate above the expected portfolio return should usually be retired before investing |
| Risk | Capacity and tolerance, established separately; knowledge and experience; behaviour in a past drawdown | See below - the lower of capacity and tolerance binds |
| Preferences and constraints | ESG or exclusion preferences, concentrated or restricted holdings, liquidity needs, currency of spending | These are constraints on the solution, not obstacles to argue around |
| Existing arrangements | Insurance policies and riders, will, CPF and SRS nominations, Lasting Power of Attorney, existing adviser arrangements | Nominations and wills are the highest-cost things to get wrong and the cheapest to fix |
| Documents | Latest account statements, last Notice of Assessment, CPF statement, insurance schedules, loan statements, any existing plan | Ask for these rather than accepting remembered figures |

## Risk capacity is not risk tolerance

Capacity is objective: time horizon, stability of income, liquidity needs, debt, dependants, and the size of the gap between spending and secure income. Tolerance is behavioural: what the household can hold through a 30% drawdown without selling.

The binding constraint is the lower of the two. A tolerance questionnaire on its own cannot justify an allocation, and a high tolerance does not create capacity that is not there. Record both, and record which one set the ceiling.

Where capacity and tolerance diverge sharply, say so explicitly. It is usually a sign the plan needs more secure income or a longer horizon rather than a compromise allocation that satisfies neither.

## The suitability test

Before anything is recommended, it should be possible to write one sentence: *this fits the household's objectives, their financial situation, their needs, and their experience with this kind of product, and they can afford the outcome if it goes wrong.*

If that sentence cannot be written, the recommendation is not ready. Alternatives considered and rejected belong in the file too - the reasoning is the work product, not just the conclusion.

## When to escalate rather than advise

- **Product-specific recommendations that require a licence.** Recommending a specific security or insurance product for a client is licensed activity in Singapore. Draft the analysis; leave the recommendation to a licensed representative of the firm.
- **US-connected persons.** US citizens, green-card holders, and US tax residents are taxed on worldwide income by the US and face US estate tax exposure on US-situs assets above a low threshold for non-residents. Do not treat these households as ordinary Singapore planning. Flag and refer.
- **Cross-border households.** Migrating, non-resident, or dual-resident households need a determination of tax residency before a plan is worth building.
- **Legal instruments.** Wills, trusts, and estate documents belong to a lawyer. You can identify that a nomination needs updating; do not draft the instrument.
- **Capacity concerns.** Signs of cognitive decline, pressure from family members, or a client who cannot follow the explanation all require a different process, not a faster one. Raise the possibility of a Lasting Power of Attorney with the client while they can still decide.
- **Anything you are not competent in.** Say so in the file. An honest gap is survivable; an invented answer is not.

## Conflicts and disclosure

Establish and disclose in writing how the adviser is paid - flat fee, assets under management, commission, trailer, or a mix - and whether any product in the recommendation pays the firm more than its alternatives. Where an in-house product is used, say so and say why. A recommendation that would change if the remuneration changed is a conflict, not a recommendation.

## What the file should show

Discovery notes and the documents relied on; the risk profile and the basis for it; every material assumption with its source; alternatives considered and why they were rejected; the recommendation and its reasoning; and the client's acknowledgement. This is what makes the work defensible years later, and it is also what makes it reviewable by a colleague.

## Red flags to surface, not smooth over

- Planned retirement spending needs a withdrawal rate the portfolio cannot support
- No cash buffer, so the household would be a forced seller in a bad market
- Concentration in a single stock, the employer's shares, or one property
- Guarantees given for someone else's debt, or any leverage
- A plan that only works if a bonus, an inheritance, or an early sale happens
- Assumptions that hold only in the favourable case
- An existing product with high surrender costs or a lock-in that has not been surfaced yet
